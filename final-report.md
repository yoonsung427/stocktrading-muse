## What drove the result

The story of this challenge is NVDA, and the last six days. The portfolio was built around AI: NVDA was the largest position (20% at open, then enlarged to ~25% by the Oct 19 rebalance), with MSFT, AMZN and META alongside it. That concentration worked while AI led the market, then turned against it all at once.

The slide was NVDA-specific at first, then became a broad rout. In order: CEO Jensen Huang's admission of infidelity and a board investigation, reports of a Justice Department inquiry and a shareholder class action, analyst downgrades (HSBC cut to hold, $375 → $170 target), then Huang's resignation on Nov 11 (interim CEO CFO Colette Kress). Even a Q3 beat-and-raise on Nov 17 (revenue $115.8B, +103% YoY; Q4 guide $130.0B) only produced an after-hours pop that faded by the next morning. The final blow came Fri Nov 20: a Reuters report that SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues at Nvidia — a reported examination, not a finding — on a day the S&P 500 fell 3.70% and the Nasdaq 5.28%, the S&P entering correction territory after a seven-day losing streak. NVDA fell 14.01% that day alone, to a new 52-week low of $124.50 intraday, closing at $125.53.

The six-day selloff into the close is what the user called it: a crazy ending. The portfolio lost $5,214.19 in the final week alone (from $47,671.32 at Fri Nov 13 close), and $2,174.75 on the final day.

What saved it from being worse: LLY (+14.04%), the pharma diversifier, was the only holding with a real gain, and GE finished marginally green. XOM's trim in October banked the only realized profit of the challenge (+$108.76) and XOM was the only holding up on the final day.

The lesson, such as it is: the Oct 19 rebalance doubled down on the exact thing that then fell apart. Concentrating a quarter of a mock portfolio in one stock — then adding to it — is a bet that the story holds for six weeks. It didn't. The benchmark, diversified across 500 names, fell too (−11.54%), but not as far.
